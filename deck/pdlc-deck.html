Product Enablement & PDLC

Unifying the Product Development Lifecycle

Peebs understanding of the mandate

The situation

Every team builds product a different way

The problem: Teams operate independently at three levels: focus area, business unit, product team. That means duplicated tooling, uneven quality, and no reliable read on when anything will ship.

Focus areas run themselves

Each area of the company operates on its own, with its own habits and years of legacy practice behind them.

Business units don't compare notes

BUs work as standalone shops. The same problem gets solved several times over, and nobody shares their learnings.

Product teams invent their own process

Every product team runs discovery, reviews, and releases its own way. Each product pod is organized differently.

The job

What we'll do

The goal

Build a modern product development lifecycle that every business unit can actually use.

Get there by making processes, templates, coaching, and tooling teams can pick up on their own. Not by forcing blind adoption.

Make it easier, don't make it required

Teams adopt a shared process when it saves them time. The frameworks and templates have to be good enough that BUs reach for them on their own.

Two parts of the solution

  • Architect: design the lifecycle and the tooling underneath it.
  • Coach: help BU leaders adopt it on their own terms.

Current Scope Understanding

Four pillars we can build off of

1. Define the lifecycle

  • One path for new products, one for improvements
  • Clear checkpoints, and who signs off at each
  • Agree what a PRD or roadmap has to include
  • Shared vocabulary across teams

2. Help teams use it

  • Playbooks and templates teams can grab
  • Onboarding and workshops, BU by BU
  • A home base for product practice
  • Get PMs across BUs talking to each other

3. Sort out the tooling

  • Settle on a preferred set of product tools
  • Automate checkpoint tracking and reporting
  • One place to find things

4. Track how it's going

  • A simple way to score where each BU stands
  • Regular checks on product and process health
  • Listen to teams, fix what isn't working
  • Compare across BUs to find what works

How it rolls out

A mock example of how we might launch this

Phase 1: Learn the landscape

  • Map how each BU works today
  • Find what's actually painful
  • Inventory the tools in use
  • Set a baseline to measure against

Phase 2: Build & pilot

  • Draft the first version of the PDLC
  • Build the core templates
  • Pick one or two willing BUs
  • Run it with them and watch closely

Phase 3: Revision & expansion

  • Rework it around what the pilot found
  • Launch the practice hub
  • Bring on the next set of BUs
  • Start the cross-BU PM group

Phase 4: The ultimate goal

  • Roll out company-wide
  • Keep scoring maturity
  • Automate portfolio reporting
  • Keep adjusting as teams push back

Measuring it

How we'd know it's working

Some preliminary ideas on how to measure adoption and success.

Are teams using it?

  • Adoption: how many BUs actually run on the shared lifecycle.
  • Maturity: whether BU scores move up over time.
  • Toolkit use: whether people open the templates and guides.

Is it getting faster?

  • Cycle time: how long discovery to launch takes.
  • Predictability: how close dates land to what was planned.
  • Ramp-up: how quickly a new PM gets productive.

Is it better to work in?

  • PM sentiment: whether the process feels clear and the tools usable.
  • Duplication: fewer one-off processes and redundant licenses.
  • Visibility: whether leadership can see the roadmap without asking.

Open questions

Some things I'm not sure about

How much authority comes with this?

Is there executive backing behind the change, or does adoption rest entirely on influence and relationships with BU leaders?

What's the budget?

What's available for product ops tooling, and for the time it takes to build the templates and practice materials?

What's the team size?

How big is the team going to be to get a PoC going?

Where does this role stop?

What belongs to this role, versus product ops or program management inside the individual product teams?
