The shared profile

One profile, built from everything they do.

  • Eight pillars, scored 0 to 400. Every chat, plan, journal entry, and game moves one of them, so progress is something a student can actually see.
  • A spider graph, not bars. The shape tells the story before any number does: strengths and growth areas in a single glance.
  • "Growth Report," not "CRI." The acronym feels like a test result. A growth report feels like something you can move.

The mentor

Always an answer, and a next step.

  • Answer first, then a next step. An open-ended "what do you think?" leaves a stuck kid more stuck, so Orchie always hands back a concrete move.
  • @ to mention, / for commands. The same patterns as the rest of Orchard, so the chat doubles as low-stakes reps for the AI fluency they'll need later.
  • The desk stays in view. Whatever a student pins gets prioritized, because that's usually what they actually came to talk about.

The engine

It builds from real work, never instead of it.

  • Drafted from what the student actually did. The bio sheet for teachers is built from 47 real observations across chats, journal, plans, and games, not a blank template.
  • Every claim links to its source. A journal entry, a plan, a quiz result. If a line doesn't sound like them, they can see why and rewrite it.
  • Orchie writes the first pass, the student owns it. Nothing leaves the desk until they've edited it. Orchie never produces the work as if it were them.

Explore

Make the abstract concrete.

  • Life Sim spends the salary, not just shows it. A student lives a virtual month on the career's pay against their real rent and bills. A number is abstract; four weeks of decisions is concrete.
  • Every path at equal weight. Four-year, bootcamp, and self-taught sit in identical cards. The layout itself says there's no default path, just paths that fit different students.
  • Local pay, not national averages. A sixteen-year-old wants to know "can I support myself doing this, here?" So we pull who's hiring and what they pay in their city.

Plan

A plan you can't freeze in front of.

  • One question at a time. Building a career plan in one sitting paralyzes a kid. As a conversation, they never see the whole mountain at once.
  • Three routes, same destination. The 2-year, 4-year, and bootcamp get identical visual weight. All three end at "employed as a Software Engineer."
  • Lands at three horizons. A 90-day push, a one-year horizon, a graduation target. A goal four years out is hard to act on; one ninety days out is something you do this week.

Learn

Skills that fit a locker break.

  • Quests under ten minutes. Anything longer feels like assigned homework. Short enough to fit between classes, so they can practice any time.
  • Every quest tagged to a pillar. "Lifts Skills" or "Lifts Focus" right on the card, so it's never a random activity. The student knows exactly why they're playing.
  • The stuff school skips. Reading a real budget, spotting a misleading chart, giving feedback without crushing someone. In every adult life, rarely in a curriculum.

Governance

Guardrails

We follow FERPA, and soon COPPA: student data stays protected, and Orchie never offers medical or therapeutic counseling.
→
  • Real-time PII scrubbing, so student records never reach the LLM.
  • Distressed-language detection that flags and escalates to a human counselor.
Our AI policy requires Orchie to cite verifiable sources and stay auditable.
→
  • Grounded in our own data plus federally compliant sources like the BLS and O*NET.
  • Every stat is cited. If Orchie doesn't know, it says so rather than hallucinate.
