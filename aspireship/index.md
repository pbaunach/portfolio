<section class="csb-problem" aria-label="The bet">
  <div class="csb-problem-inner">
    <div class="csb-problem-copy">
      <h2 class="csb-problem-question">How do you help someone switch careers when their resume doesn&rsquo;t reflect their future potential?</h2>
      <p class="csb-problem-body">Traditional hiring filters out career changers long before they get to speak with a manager. Candidates feel stuck with a resume that no longer represents them, and employers are wary of hiring without proven experience. Aspireship&rsquo;s model had to deliver three things simultaneously: teach the core skills, generate real proof of work, and build trust between both sides to make the hire happen.</p>
    </div>
    <ul class="csb-stat-list">
      <li class="csb-stat">
        <span class="csb-stat-lead">45% &rarr; 89%</span>
        <div class="csb-stat-body">

        </div>
      </li>
      <li class="csb-stat">
        <span class="csb-stat-lead">3% &rarr; 15%</span>
        <div class="csb-stat-body">
          <p class="csb-stat-text">Paid conversion funnel after I ungated the first module for a real try-before-you-buy experience</p>
        </div>
      </li>
      <li class="csb-stat">
        <span class="csb-stat-lead">300+</span>
        <div class="csb-stat-body">
          <p class="csb-stat-text">Candidates placed with employers through the matching system and algorithm I designed</p>
        </div>
      </li>
    </ul>

<section class="csb-band" id="learn" aria-label="learn the job">
  <div class="csb-band-head">
    <div class="csb-band-inner">
      <h2 class="csb-section-title">Learn the Job</h2>
    </div>
  </div>
  <div class="csb-band-inner csb-band-body">
    <p class="csb-band-lead">The course had two jobs at once: teach SaaS sales to someone who has never sold software, and generate proof that they can do it. Getting people to finish was the hard part, and it turned out to be a design problem rather than a content problem.</p>

      <div class="csb-row">
        <figure class="cs-callout-figure csb-row-media">
          <img src="{{ '/assets/img/aspireship-screens/Course-01.png' | relative_url }}" alt="Aspireship course overview with the first section unlocked">
        </figure>
        <div class="csb-row-notes">
          <p class="csb-note">Section 1 is unlocked for everyone. We were asking people to buy a career change before they knew whether they would even like the work. Giving away the first module changed the question from "is this worth the money" to "do I want to keep going."</p>
          <p class="csb-note">The upgrade prompt sits at the end of the free section rather than on a splash screen at signup. By the time anyone reaches this button they have finished four lessons and two quizzes. The ask lands after the value, not before it.</p>
          <p class="csb-note">Locked lessons stay visible with their titles readable. Hiding them would have been tidier, but seeing "The Demo" and "Handling Objections" sitting behind the lock is what makes the upgrade concrete instead of abstract.</p>
        </div>
      </div>

      <div class="csb-row">
        <figure class="cs-callout-figure csb-row-media">
          <img src="{{ '/assets/img/aspireship-screens/Course-02.png' | relative_url }}" alt="Aspireship lesson player with transcript and lesson list">
        </figure>
        <div class="csb-row-notes">
          <p class="csb-note"><strong class="csb-note-lead">Lesson view</strong> Video, transcript, study questions, and resources under one player, with the full lesson list on the right so the end is always in sight. Section 1 closes with a recorded roleplay project and a self-assessment, which is where students stalled out of fear of failing. Practice runs, and saying plainly up front that the assessment could be retaken, is what got them through it.</p>
        </div>
      </div>

      <div class="csb-row">
        <figure class="cs-callout-figure csb-row-media">
          <img src="{{ '/assets/img/aspireship-screens/Learning-Paths-02.png' | relative_url }}" alt="Aspireship outbounding learning path detail">
        </figure>
        <div class="csb-row-notes">
          <p class="csb-note"><strong class="csb-note-lead">A single path</strong> "Up Next" is the whole point: one clear action with everything else collapsed beneath it. "Things to think about" pushes into Community, so a path has somewhere to lead besides the next video.</p>
        </div>
      </div>

      <div class="csb-row">
        <figure class="cs-callout-figure csb-row-media">
          <img src="{{ '/assets/img/aspireship-screens/Video-Library-01.png' | relative_url }}" alt="Aspireship video library home">
        </figure>
        <div class="csb-row-notes">
          <p class="csb-note"><strong class="csb-note-lead">Video library home</strong> Live events, latest additions, and category browsing. This was the retention surface: a reason to open Aspireship during a week when you were not actively taking a course.</p>
        </div>
      </div>

      <div class="csb-row">
        <figure class="cs-callout-figure csb-row-media">
          <img src="{{ '/assets/img/aspireship-screens/Video-Library-02.png' | relative_url }}" alt="Aspireship video detail with transcript and similar videos">
        </figure>
        <div class="csb-row-notes">
          <p class="csb-note"><strong class="csb-note-lead">Video detail</strong> Description, speaker bio, and transcript in tabs. "Team Playlists" and "Assigned to You" appear in the rail for enterprise seats, so one library serves both an individual career changer and a sales manager onboarding a team.</p>
        </div>
      </div>
  </div>
</section>

<section class="csb-band" id="match" aria-label="get matched">
  <div class="csb-band-head">
    <div class="csb-band-inner">
      <h2 class="csb-section-title">Get Matched</h2>
    </div>
  </div>
  <div class="csb-band-inner csb-band-body">
    <p class="csb-band-lead">This is where the product stopped being a course and became a marketplace, and where the business model actually lived: matching is what made customer acquisition cheap. Two sides with opposite fears, the candidate afraid of being judged on the resume they are trying to leave behind and the employer afraid of hiring someone who has never done the job. The match had to move both past that at the same time.</p>

      <h3 class="csb-sub-title">The Candidate</h3>
      <div class="csb-row">
        <figure class="cs-callout-figure csb-row-media">
          <img src="{{ '/assets/img/aspireship-screens/Candidate-02.png' | relative_url }}" alt="Aspireship candidate matches with double opt-in status">
        </figure>
        <div class="csb-row-notes">
          <p class="csb-note">The candidate answers first. A match never reaches the employer until the candidate has said they are interested, so nobody gets quietly shopped around while they are still employed somewhere else.</p>
          <p class="csb-note">Three states on one track: waiting on you, waiting on them, mutual interest. Job seekers read silence as rejection, so the interface always says out loud which side the ball is on.</p>
          <p class="csb-note">Base and OTE sit on the card before the click. Compensation is the first thing a sales candidate screens on, and burying it behind "View More" only wasted both sides' time.</p>
          <p class="csb-note">Match history stays on the page instead of clearing out. Past matches are the proof that the pipeline is real, which matters most during the quiet weeks when there are no new ones.</p>
        </div>
      </div>

      <div class="csb-row">
        <figure class="cs-callout-figure csb-row-media">
          <img src="{{ '/assets/img/aspireship-screens/Candidate-01.png' | relative_url }}" alt="Aspireship candidate match profile setup">
        </figure>
        <div class="csb-row-notes">
          <p class="csb-note"><strong class="csb-note-lead">Match profile setup</strong> A completion checklist rather than a wall of form fields, plus an explicit promise that preferences like salary goal and company size are never shown to employers. Candidates would not answer these honestly until we said so on the screen itself.</p>
        </div>
      </div>

      <div class="csb-row">
        <figure class="cs-callout-figure csb-row-media">
          <img src="{{ '/assets/img/aspireship-screens/Candidate-03.png' | relative_url }}" alt="Aspireship candidate profile as seen by an employer">
        </figure>
        <div class="csb-row-notes">
          <p class="csb-note"><strong class="csb-note-lead">The profile an employer actually sees</strong> A recorded introduction, an unscripted roleplay clip, the Aspireship courses completed, and the resume last. Career changers lose on the resume, so we led with the closest thing to watching someone do the job. Disclosing the project guidelines was deliberate: employers only judge a raw clip fairly when they know it was unrehearsed.</p>
        </div>
      </div>

      <h3 class="csb-sub-title">The Employer</h3>
      <div class="csb-row">
        <figure class="cs-callout-figure csb-row-media">
          <img src="{{ '/assets/img/aspireship-screens/Employer-02.png' | relative_url }}" alt="Aspireship employer candidate profile criteria">
        </figure>
        <div class="csb-row-notes">
          <p class="csb-note">The question the entire business depended on. Every employer had to answer it explicitly before matching began, which turned "we're open to non-traditional candidates" from a nice sentiment in a sales call into a filter the matching could actually use.</p>
          <p class="csb-note">Asked separately from the first, because "no SaaS experience" and "never held this title" are different risks. Plenty of employers would accept one and not the other, and collapsing them into a single question cost us real matches.</p>
          <p class="csb-note">Employers choose attributes of a person who succeeds in the role instead of years of experience. Our entire roster was career changers, so a conventional requirements checklist would have filtered out every last one of them.</p>
          <p class="csb-note">Match criteria live as a tab on the job, not in a separate setup flow. The hiring manager is already here writing the post, and asking in the same place is the only reason this ever got filled in.</p>
        </div>
      </div>

      <div class="csb-row">
        <figure class="cs-callout-figure csb-row-media">
          <img src="{{ '/assets/img/aspireship-screens/Employer-01.png' | relative_url }}" alt="Aspireship employer match queue">
        </figure>
        <div class="csb-row-notes">
          <p class="csb-note"><strong class="csb-note-lead">The employer's match queue</strong> Staff Picks first, with the Graduate badge and Aspireship achievements on every card. For a candidate with no relevant work history, that badge is the entire credential, so it gets more visual weight than anything except the name.</p>
        </div>
      </div>

      <div class="csb-row">
        <figure class="cs-callout-figure csb-row-media">
          <img src="{{ '/assets/img/aspireship-screens/Employer-03.png' | relative_url }}" alt="Aspireship employer job list with approval statuses">
        </figure>
        <div class="csb-row-notes">
          <p class="csb-note"><strong class="csb-note-lead">Job list with a real approval workflow</strong> Draft, Pending Review, Changes Requested, Open, Closed. Enterprise teams needed a hiring manager to draft a role and an admin to approve it before it could go live, so status had to be a first-class column.</p>
        </div>
      </div>
  </div>
</section>

<section class="csb-band" id="operate" aria-label="operate the market">
  <div class="csb-band-head">
    <div class="csb-band-inner">
      <h2 class="csb-section-title">Operate the Market</h2>
    </div>
  </div>
  <div class="csb-band-inner csb-band-body">
    <p class="csb-band-lead">Marketplaces do not run themselves. Behind both customer-facing sides sat the tooling our own placement team lived in all day, and it is where the honest picture of the business showed up.</p>

      <div class="csb-row">
        <figure class="cs-callout-figure csb-row-media">
          <img src="{{ '/assets/img/aspireship-screens/Internal-01.png' | relative_url }}" alt="Aspireship internal admin dashboard">
        </figure>
        <div class="csb-row-notes">
          <p class="csb-note">Three numbers the placement team works from every morning: assessments to review, candidates ready, jobs to fill. Everything else on the page is context for those three.</p>
          <p class="csb-note">83% of candidates inactive for 30 days or more. This single row reframed the roadmap. Our bottleneck was never supply, it was that people go quiet, and the "Interested in new job matches?" toggle on the candidate profile came directly out of this number.</p>
          <p class="csb-note">Placement count sits immediately under the inactive count on purpose. Seen alone, 20 placements looks like a win. Seen against 4,223 candidates, it is the actual state of the business.</p>
          <p class="csb-note">"Jobs filled not by us" gets its own line. Nobody enjoys a dashboard that counts losses, but a marketplace that only measures wins cannot tell the difference between a bad match and a slow one.</p>
        </div>
      </div>

      <div class="csb-row">
        <figure class="cs-callout-figure csb-row-media">
          <img src="{{ '/assets/img/aspireship-screens/Internal-02.png' | relative_url }}" alt="Aspireship internal job record with activity log">
        </figure>
        <div class="csb-row-notes">
          <p class="csb-note"><strong class="csb-note-lead">Internal job record</strong> Every job carries an activity log (posted, match added, interested, hired) attributed to a person. Placements played out over weeks and across multiple staff, so the handoff history was the feature.</p>
        </div>
      </div>

      <div class="csb-row">
        <figure class="cs-callout-figure csb-row-media">
          <img src="{{ '/assets/img/aspireship-screens/Internal-03.png' | relative_url }}" alt="Aspireship internal match suggestions with reasons">
        </figure>
        <div class="csb-row-notes">
          <p class="csb-note"><strong class="csb-note-lead">Match suggestions with reasons</strong> The system proposes, a human decides. "Why it's a match" has to read plainly before anyone acts on it, and we deliberately never shipped silent automated matching between the two sides.</p>
        </div>
      </div>
  </div>
</section>

      <li class="csb-reflect-card">
        <h3 class="csb-reflect-title">Turning a vague problem into a measured one.</h3>
        <p class="csb-reflect-desc">Built the shared baseline with Marketing and Engineering, mapped the conversion funnel, and designed against the two drop-off points it exposed.</p>
